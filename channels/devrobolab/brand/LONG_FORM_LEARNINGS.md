# DevRoboLab - long-form learnings from one Hinglish AI/money explainer creator (studied 2026-11-05)

Source: 3 LONG videos (16:19, 17:23, 19:03; 480p, 16:9) of one creator who explains AI and money topics in Hinglish, studied under RULES 12c and
brand/LEARNING_PROCESS.md. All downloads, frames and transcripts were deleted on 2026-11-05 (see research/study_log.md). Only abstract principles and
numbers are kept; no wording, titles, names, frames or jokes are reused. Numbers are rough (frame samples + one full Whisper transcript of video 1;
the transcripts of videos 2-3 did not finish in time, so their structure is read from contact sheets only).

## Numbers (measured)
- Length 16-19 min (ours: 3-8 min, so scale section counts down, keep ratios). Voice about 185 words/min in Hinglish, 14 pauses over 0.6 s in 16 min: dense but with a breath at each section change.
- Visual-change events (frame difference > 0.08, includes card animations): 12, 21 and 17 per minute. Real hard cuts (> 0.25) only 1-2 per minute: the picture changes mostly by cross-dissolve, card entry or a new b-roll inside a dark frame.
- Loudness -14.1 to -14.8 LUFS, loudness range only 2-2.5 LU: voice-forward and flat, music (if any) is a low bed with no swells under speech.
- Full face-cam is only about 5-10% of the runtime (intro, hard-point moments, one plug); the rest is b-roll, cards, screen recordings and quoted source clips. A small face bubble (about 10% of frame width, rounded) sometimes floats in a corner over screen recordings.
- On-screen text events: a new text card, pill or number about every 4-8 s; word-by-word lower captions all the time.

## (a) Hook, 0-90 s
- 0-10 s: a "you are in this scene" picture with a tension line (everyone looks the same, you cannot tell the difference), no greeting, no channel intro.
- 10-35 s: the same scene sharpened with a concrete NUMBER gap (two people, same income, very different result after N years). The question is posed as a bet: "who is richer?"
- 35-70 s: the promise is made in plain words and as an on-screen "in this video" list (3 items with numbers 1-2-3), then one line that rewards staying to the end. The promise is stated twice in slightly different words.
- 70-85 s: one short credibility/who-I-am line, only after the hook; then "let's go". First real payoff (first number reveal) comes about 90-110 s; the first tension was already planted at 5-15 s.

## (b) Structure and chapters
- One running story with two named example people (a "looks rich" one and a "is rich" one) carries the whole video; each section adds one number to their story and ends on a small twist. This makes 16 min feel like one story, not 6 tips.
- 5-7 sections of about 2-3 min each, each opened by a small label card (for example "THEN", "AFTER 10 YEARS", "THE QUESTION", "THE 7 STEPS") and closed by a one-line rule.
- Order: relatable scene, the hidden number, the myth that excuses it, the correct tool use (nuance, not a ban), the full calculation, a target/rule, the real lesson ("the one thing that matters"), a 3-step action list for today.
- Linking: each section starts from the last line of the one before ("now see what the second person did"). A mid-video recap is a short check-list card, not a speech.
- Ending (about last 90 s): action list (3 items, do today), the one core sentence, one soft plug for their paid workshop (card + "link in description"), a gated like/subscribe ask, a comment question that makes the viewer pick one of the two characters, a sources table (5-6 rows: source name, fact used, date), then 15-20 s of end screen with two video slots and a subscribe slot on a black bar.
- The other two videos are "case study" form: a news peg, a quote from a real public talk shown as a lower-third clip with the speaker's name and role, then a numbered list of steps (7 steps as a row of icons) and a live screen recording of the tool for the middle third.

## (c) Explanation style
- Numbers before concepts: every claim becomes a number in a glass card (big figure, tiny label) within 1-2 s of being said. Example arithmetic is shown end to end ("full calculation"), not only the answer.
- One analogy per hard idea, built as a simple animated diagram in the same style (a phone with layers: app, operating system, chip, mapped onto an AI agent, model and a skill). The analogy is held on screen 15-25 s and re-used later with one element highlighted.
- Jargon rule: the English term appears in a small pill above the picture the first time ("a skill - no coding"), then is used normally. Hindi/English mix is natural; terms stay in English.
- Repetition: the key sentence is said three times in different forms (promise, definition, recap). Questions to the viewer ("what do you see here?") come as a rhetorical beat before the reveal.
- Two-column compare cards with a red cross and a green tick (wrong way vs right way) for every misconception; a check-list card with ticks for criteria.
- Nuance over scare: the myth is never "banned", it is shown when it is good and when it is bad (two checkmark rows), which builds trust.

## (d) Visuals
- Backdrop: near-black with slight warm gradient; one accent colour (lime yellow-green) used for the key word in captions, card borders, ticks, tags. No second accent except red for cross/warning.
- Glass cards: dark translucent rounded rectangles with thin lime border and soft glow; one big number (about 7-9% of frame height), a tiny label under it; two cards side by side for comparisons; check-list rows (3 rows, tick on the left, 2.5-3% height text).
- B-roll: stock footage of real situations, shown as an inset rounded "window" (about 70-75% of frame width, never full-bleed) on the black page, with a tiny pill label above it (2-5 words, about 1.5% height) and a tiny credit line under it. When a card appears, the b-roll stays as a darkened blurred backdrop (dim to 20-30%).
- Quoted sources: screenshot of an article with the key sentence highlighted in yellow marker; a speaker clip from an official talk with a name tag (name + role + event), plus a small "source" line. Screen recordings of the real tool (dark UI, 2-4 s each, cursor visible) fill the how-to part.
- Lower captions: bold sans, 2-4% of frame height, 2-4 words at once, word by word, the current word bright and the rest dim; key word tinted lime. Always at the same spot (about 88-92% height), centre.
- Zoom/highlight: slow push-in on a source screenshot, yellow marker highlight on the key sentence, blur of the previous frame behind a new card; dissolves, no wipes.

## (e) Editing rhythm
- Average shot 3-5 s; text cards stay 2-4 s, diagrams 10-25 s but build in steps (a new node or line every 2-3 s, so the screen is never still). No shot over 8 s without something changing.
- Transitions: cross-dissolve and blur-through only. No shake, no speed ramps, no glitch. Hard cuts only between face-cam and b-roll.
- Sound: voice clean and loud, a low steady music bed, few soft whooshes/clicks on card entry (barely audible); silence of 0.5-1 s before a key number.
- Pace changes at section changes: a short breath, a label card, then speed up again.

## (f) Retention devices
- Open loop at 10-35 s (who is richer?) closed only in the last third; a second small loop at each section end ("but the next thing is the real mistake").
- "In this video" list at the start and a "step N of M" list later; a promised-reward line near the start for those who stay to the end.
- Pattern interrupts every 60-90 s: face-cam cut, a different visual type (real screenshot instead of card), a new label card, a "now check this" pill above the b-roll.
- A live-workshop plug appears twice (middle and end) as a calm card, not a hard sell; comment question forces a choice (A or B).

## (g) Title, thumbnail, description (pattern only)
- Titles about 9-14 words, one bold claim plus a "do this" or "here is what to do" half; a number or a time frame in brackets; strong emotional stake (money, job, being left behind). Mixed Hinglish/English spelling in Roman script.
- Thumbnail (seen only via the video frames, so low confidence): one face with strong emotion plus 2-4 big words; high contrast, dark with one bright accent.
- Description structure: one-paragraph promise, chapter list with times, link to their paid workshop at the top, sources list. Chapters exist in every video.

## (h) Weak or not worth copying
- Whisper-visible repeated promise (same promise stated twice in a row) wastes 10-15 s; say it once.
- Long stock b-roll that only decorates (hands, coins, calculators) while the voice makes a point; it does not explain anything. Use a diagram or a number instead.
- Very small tags (about 1.5% of frame height) and tiny source credits are unreadable on a phone; our minimum is 2.5-3% for any text.
- 16-19 min runtime with a paid-workshop plug: wrong for a 3-8 min news digest; do not copy the sales plug.
- Heavy dark, dim and glass look is theirs; ours is white/black/yellow marker with the robot.

## Map to DevRoboLab (ranked by impact / effort)
1. HOOK = scene + number gap (high / low): open the digest with ONE concrete before/after or number contrast from item 1 (0-8 s), then the 3-item "today you will know" list on screen (2.5% x 3 rows) by 15 s, no robot intro line. Engine: existing statement + list layers; effort: script only.
2. Running example person/object (high / medium): for explainers, carry ONE named everyday character (for example a shopkeeper or a student) through all items; each item adds one number or step to their story. Script pattern only; effort: low.
3. Big-number card per claim (high / low): a white card with one big figure (7-9% height), tiny label, yellow border; two-card compare with cross and tick. Engine: HTML/SVG card layer already possible in landscape decks; add a reusable 'numcard' and 'compare' template, effort: medium.
4. Section label card + one-line rule closing each item (medium / low): 'ITEM 2 . WHY IT MATTERS' pill in the ticker position, plus a 2-line rule card at the end of each item; adds chapters feeling without extra time.
5. Diagram analogy held 15 s and built in steps (high / medium): one analogy per explainer drawn with our draw layer (nodes appear every 2-3 s), reused in the recap with one node highlighted.
6. Pattern interrupt every 60-90 s (medium / low): rotate visual type (card, symbol scene, screenshot of source page, robot reaction, draw layer); never the same layout twice in a row.
7. Source on screen (medium / low): quoted official page screenshot with the key sentence highlighted in yellow marker, a small source line (3% height), and a sources table card before the ending; this also fits our 'page me nahi likha' rule and builds trust.
8. Ending stack (medium / low): action list of 2-3 items to do today, one core sentence, closing line A (CTA_VOICE/CTA_SCREEN/CTA_PILL), a comment question that asks the viewer to choose A or B, then 10-15 s end-screen area (two video slots, subscribe slot) with no essential picture there.
9. Captions word by word (medium / medium): lower captions 3% height, current word bright, key word yellow; only for long videos where we have word times (scripts/yb_wordtimes.py).
10. Flat loudness: voice about -14 LUFS, music very low, 1 s of silence before key numbers (low / low).
- Notes only (TTS now): face-cam bursts every 90-120 s and a small face bubble over screen recordings would work if the user's own cloned voice or face is added later.

## Do not copy
- Their dark glass look as a whole (dark page + lime border + b-roll in a rounded inset + bottom word captions + 'Watch next' end card): we may use the principles (number cards, inset sources, compare cards) but only in our white/black/yellow marker style with the robot.
- Their characters, example numbers, section order of the money story, title/thumbnail phrasing, workshop plug and any clips, speaker footage or stock footage.
