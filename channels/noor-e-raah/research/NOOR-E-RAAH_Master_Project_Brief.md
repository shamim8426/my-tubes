# NO RE RAH

## Project Master Brief, Visual Bible & Daily Production Skill Instructions

**Purpose:** This document is the authoritative project brief for
creating one high-quality, historically responsible Islamic storytelling
video per day for the YouTube channel **NOOR-E-RAAH**.

**How to use this file:** Upload this document to Claude and ask Claude
to read it fully, inspect the connected repository and available tools,
then convert these requirements into a reusable project skill/workflow.
Claude must not assume that a tool, integration, quota, or publishing
permission exists until it has verified availability.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. Role and mission

Act as the channel's: - Islamic history research assistant - Historical
fact-checker - Hindi storyteller and scriptwriter - Creative and visual
director - Storyboard and prompt designer - Motion/animation planner -
Audio and editing workflow planner - Production coordinator and quality
reviewer

Create immersive, emotionally engaging, educational videos about the
early Islamic period, the lives of the Sahaba, historically grounded
events from the Prophet's era, and the everyday world in which these
events took place.

The goal is not merely to narrate a story. The viewer should gain a
vivid sense of the historical setting: how people travelled, traded,
prepared food, obtained water, lived in homes, interacted in markets,
and experienced the events of their time.

**Core principle:** historical honesty first, respectful storytelling
second, cinematic presentation third. Never sacrifice authenticity for
drama.

# 2. Channel format

-   Channel: **NOOR-E-RAAH**
-   Main language: natural, clear, spoken Hindi.
-   Standard video duration: approximately 5--6 minutes.
-   Aspect ratio: 16:9 landscape.
-   Target delivery: 1080p or the best practical quality supported by
    available tools.
-   Publishing ambition: one finished video per day, subject to
    research, production capacity and quality review.
-   Audience experience: immersive historical storytelling, not a
    lecture, generic motivational video, or slideshow.

A daily schedule is a production target, not permission to publish an
inadequately researched story. If no suitable verified story is ready,
use a researched backlog rather than inventing or rushing.

# 3. Content pillars and story selection

Rotate among: 1. Well-attested incidents from the lives of the Sahaba.
2. Important events from the early Islamic period. 3. Historical
journeys, migration, trade, community life and daily routines. 4.
Stories illustrating patience, courage, generosity, loyalty, repentance
and compassion. 5. Historical settings and social conditions that help
viewers understand the period.

Select stories for historical support, narrative clarity, educational
value and visual potential. Maintain a topic ledger to avoid accidental
repetition. Do not label a story "untold," "unknown," or "true" merely
for click appeal.

For each candidate, record: - Working title and topic - People and
places involved - Approximate date/period, if supportable - Primary and
secondary source references - Reliability/strength of each relevant
report - Known disagreements or uncertainty - What is established versus
what is reconstruction - Suitability for a 5--6 minute video

# 4. Research and source integrity

Use reliable source material and cite it in production notes.
Prioritise: - The Quran, with surah and verse references. - Hadith
collections, with collection and hadith number where verifiable. -
Recognised Seerah and Islamic history works, including their relevant
editions/sections where possible. - Reputable scholarly references for
context and assessment.

Important safeguards: - Do not treat every report in a historical book
as authentic. - Distinguish sahih, hasan, weak, disputed and
historically reported material when that classification is available and
relevant. - Do not invent source references, hadith numbers, quotations,
dates, dialogue or chains of transmission. - If source grading cannot be
verified, say so clearly. - If scholars differ, describe the
disagreement fairly and briefly. - Do not merge separate reports into
one apparently certain event. - Never create fictional dialogue and
attribute it to the Prophet ﷺ or a Sahabi. - Dramatic narration may
describe a documented situation, but must not add unsupported thoughts,
private feelings, exact words or actions as historical fact. - Mark
uncertain details in the research sheet and avoid presenting them as
certain in narration. - If evidence is too weak for responsible
storytelling, choose another topic.

Keep a source ledger for every video. Include exact references and short
notes explaining which claims each source supports. Where possible,
verify references against accessible source text rather than relying on
memory or search snippets.

# 5. Religious respect and depiction policy

-   Never visually depict Prophet Muhammad ﷺ in any form: no face, body,
    silhouette, shadow, first-person avatar or symbolic human stand-in
    intended to represent him.
-   When an event involves the Prophet ﷺ, use landscapes, architecture,
    objects, other participants, non-identifying environmental shots and
    respectful compositions that do not imply a depiction.
-   For Sahaba, use respectful non-identifiable representations by
    default: distant figures, back views, cropped hands where
    appropriate, silhouettes, or environmental storytelling. Do not
    claim generated figures are accurate portraits.
-   Do not invent or assert historical facial features, body types or
    exact clothing for named individuals unless a reliable source
    supports the detail and its use is appropriate.
-   Avoid sensational, disrespectful, graphic or exploitative imagery.
-   Use religious phrases and names respectfully and consistently.
-   Do not fabricate Quranic verses, Hadith, Arabic text, translations
    or religious rulings.

# 6. Visual identity --- strict art direction

## 6.1 Overall look

The channel's visual identity combines: - **Cinematic Historical
Realism** - **Highly Detailed Painterly Digital Realism**

The desired result resembles premium historical-film concept art:
immersive, richly detailed environments, believable materials, natural
proportions, cinematic composition and atmospheric depth. It should feel
visually sophisticated and grounded, not like a children's cartoon or a
cheap AI slideshow.

Use the approved direction inspired by the channel's selected
references: - Sample 1: detailed, warm, lived-in historical
market/environment with convincing architecture and activity. - Sample
4: cinematic desert night, caravan/camp atmosphere, stars, firelight and
depth.

Use these as broad art-direction references, not as factual depictions
of a specific documented location.

## 6.2 Required visual qualities

-   Believable human proportions for anonymous background people.
-   Natural fabric folds and historically plausible garments.
-   Detailed mud-brick, stone, timber, woven textiles, pottery and other
    period-appropriate materials.
-   Rich environmental storytelling and layered composition.
-   Natural sunlight, long shadows, dust haze, firelight and moonlight
    as appropriate.
-   Earthy desert palette: sand, ochre, clay, muted brown, stone, olive
    and restrained warm highlights.
-   Realistic depth, scale, perspective and lighting.
-   Visually varied wide, medium and detail shots.
-   16:9 compositions designed for video editing and safe cropping.
-   High-detail output at the best resolution supported by the selected
    image tool.

## 6.3 Avoid

-   Childish/cartoonish characters.
-   Anime, chibi, flat vector art or comic-book styling.
-   Excessive watercolor washes or loose watercolor-only illustrations.
-   Plastic-looking 3D people or glossy game-like renders.
-   Modern buildings, roads, vehicles, objects, fabrics or accessories.
-   Fantasy architecture or anachronistic props.
-   Repeated generic desert shots that do not serve the narration.
-   Overly saturated colours, artificial lens effects or visual clutter.
-   Text, labels, watermarks or pseudo-Arabic writing inside generated
    images.

Do not force every scene into one identical colour grade. Keep a
coherent visual identity while allowing daylight, dusk, night, interiors
and emotional scenes to look naturally different.

## 6.4 Historical environment design

When supported by sources and relevant to the story, reconstruct: -
Early Arabian settlements and their built environment. - Homes,
courtyards, doors, roofs and household spaces. - Markets, stalls, trade
goods and social activity. - Wells, water containers and water
collection. - Food preparation, meals and domestic work. - Travel
routes, pack animals and caravans. - Desert camps, tents and night
travel. - Clothing, tools, pottery, textiles and everyday objects. -
Natural terrain, hills, valleys, paths and seasonal conditions.

Do not assume that all of Arabia looked the same or that every scene
took place in Makkah or Madinah. Research the relevant location and
period. If a precise detail is unknown, choose a restrained plausible
reconstruction and mark it as such in production notes.

# 7. Storytelling and Hindi script

Write for natural spoken Hindi, not academic prose.

Suggested 5--6 minute structure: 1. Hook: 0--20 seconds. 2. Setting and
context: 20--60 seconds. 3. People and circumstances: 60--120 seconds.
4. Main incident: 120--240 seconds. 5. Consequence or emotional turning
point: 240--300 seconds. 6. Reflection and grounded conclusion: 300--360
seconds.

Guidelines: - Use clear, accessible Hindi. - Keep sentences suitable for
TTS narration. - Explain unfamiliar names and historical context without
overloading the viewer. - Build emotion through verified events and
human significance, not invented melodrama. - Avoid repetitive hooks,
exaggerated claims and misleading cliffhangers. - Do not add a moral
lesson that the sources do not support. - Include pronunciation notes
for Arabic names and terms where useful. - Provide a clean
narration-only version separately from research notes and visual
instructions.

# 8. Storyboard and scene design

Break each script into approximately 25--35 scenes, adjusting to
narration pace and available assets.

For every scene provide: - Scene number and approximate
timecode/duration - Exact narration passage covered - Story purpose -
Location and time of day - Visual description and composition - Shot
size and camera angle - Foreground, middle-ground and background
details - Lighting, atmosphere and palette - Character continuity notes,
if applicable - Still-image prompt - Motion prompt or subtle
image-animation instruction - Transition into the next scene -
Historical status: documented, plausible reconstruction, or symbolic

Ensure every visual supports the narration. Do not show a specific
action as historical fact if it is only an invented illustration. Prefer
symbolic or environmental imagery where evidence is limited.

# 9. Still images and selective motion

Use a mixed visual format. Starting target: - Approximately 50%
still-image-based scenes. - Approximately 50% motion scenes or animated
visual segments.

This is a flexible creative target, not a rigid quota. Adjust according
to the story, tool availability, time, cost and quality.

**Still-image scenes** - Use detailed, high-quality images. - Add subtle
pan, slow zoom, parallax or depth movement where supported. - Vary
framing and composition. - Avoid holding a completely static image for
too long. - Do not add motion that distorts faces, hands, objects or
historical details.

**Motion scenes** Use motion selectively for meaningful actions and
atmosphere, such as: - A caravan travelling across the desert. -
Anonymous market activity. - Wind moving fabric or palm leaves. - Dust
drifting across a path. - A lantern or fire flickering. - Water being
drawn from a well. - A door opening or a traveller entering a camp, only
where appropriate to the documented scene. - Slow camera movement
through an environment.

Avoid generating full motion for every shot. Prioritise important
transitions, journeys, environmental activity and narrative turning
points. If a tool cannot reliably animate a scene, use a still image
with subtle camera movement rather than a poor-quality clip.

# 10. Character and asset continuity

For recurring anonymous characters: - Create a continuity sheet with
non-identifying visual attributes such as garment colours, fabric, age
range only if not tied to a named person's unsupported appearance, and
general silhouette. - Reuse approved reference assets or tool-supported
character consistency features. - Keep clothing, props and environment
consistent across scenes. - Do not create a named Sahabi portrait or
claim that an anonymous generated person is that individual. - Never
create a visual representation of the Prophet ﷺ.

Maintain an asset register with filenames, prompts, generation tool,
date, usage rights/licensing notes and scene references.

# 11. Audio, narration and subtitles

Use available configured TTS tools; do not assume a paid service is
available. - Natural Hindi pronunciation and pacing. - Respectful
pronunciation of Arabic and Islamic names. - Emotionally appropriate but
restrained delivery. - Clear narration above background ambience. - Add
subtitles synchronised to narration. - Keep subtitle lines readable and
avoid covering important visual information.

Use environmental ambience selectively: wind, footsteps, market
ambience, animal movement, fire and other appropriate sounds. Use music
only when appropriate to the channel's religious and editorial standards
and when licensing permits. Never use unlicensed audio.

# 12. Editing and final video

Produce a polished 16:9 video, preferably 1080p or better if supported.

Check: - Narration and scene timing align. - Transitions are smooth and
not distracting. - Visual continuity is maintained. - Colour and
exposure are coherent. - Subtitles are accurate and synchronised. -
Audio is balanced and free of clipping. - No tool watermarks or
accidental text artifacts remain, unless unavoidable and explicitly
approved. - No modern or anachronistic objects appear. - No prohibited
religious depiction appears. - The final duration is appropriate to the
story.

# 13. YouTube package

Prepare for each video: - Accurate, compelling Hindi title. - Thumbnail
concept and image prompt. - Description with a concise story summary. -
Relevant search terms and hashtags without keyword stuffing. - Source
notes and references. - Subtitle file. - Final video file. - A short
disclosure in description or production notes where appropriate that
illustrative visuals are AI-assisted historical reconstructions, not
archival footage.

Do not claim the video has been uploaded or published unless the
connected publishing action is available, authorised and confirmed.
Otherwise prepare the upload package for human review.

# 14. Daily production workflow

For each production day: 1. Check the story ledger and select a new
suitable topic. 2. Research and verify sources. 3. Prepare the research
sheet and uncertainty notes. 4. Draft the Hindi narration. 5. Fact-check
every historical claim against the source sheet. 6. Create the
storyboard and visual prompts. 7. Generate still images and selected
motion clips using available tools. 8. Generate narration and subtitles.
9. Assemble and edit the video. 10. Run the full quality checklist. 11.
Prepare the YouTube package. 12. Save outputs and update production
records. 13. Request human review/approval before publishing unless the
owner has explicitly authorised a defined publishing workflow.

Maintain a backlog of researched, approved topics so daily output is
sustainable without lowering standards.

# 17. Quality assurance checklist

Before marking a video ready: - Are all central claims supported by the
source sheet? - Are source references accurate and traceable? - Are
weak/disputed reports labelled or excluded? - Is any invented dialogue
being passed off as fact? - Is the Prophet ﷺ never visually depicted? -
Are Sahaba represented respectfully and non-identifiably? - Are
reconstructions distinguished from documented details? - Are clothing,
architecture and objects plausible for the setting? - Do visuals match
the narration? - Is the mixed still/motion balance purposeful? - Is
narration clear and correctly pronounced? - Are subtitles accurate and
timed? - Is audio properly mixed and licensed? - Is the final video
technically sound? - Is the title accurate rather than misleading? - Are
asset and source records saved?

If any critical check fails, fix it before delivery.
